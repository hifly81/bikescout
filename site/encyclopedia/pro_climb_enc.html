Categorization

Core Concept: Heuristic Vertical Intensity

In cycling, not all meters climbed are equal. A 500m ascent on a paved road feels different from 500m on rough off-road terrain. BikeScout classifies significant climbs with a transparent heuristic score based on elevation gain, distance and average gradient. These labels are planning aids, not official UCI race classifications.

How BikeScout Detects Climbs

Climb detection starts from the route elevation profile. BikeScout smooths elevation, identifies ascending segments, and keeps climbs that are meaningful enough to affect pacing, nutrition and bike setup. Very small bumps are ignored to avoid noisy micro-categories.

The Heuristic Score

BikeScout computes a climb score from ascent and average gradient. The score is used as a practical planning signal:

Score = (Total_Gain_m * Avg_Gradient_percent) / 10
Category is assigned from score and gain thresholds (HC/Cat 1-4)
category_system = "bikescout_heuristic"

For terrain analysis, BikeScout also uses a FIETS-style index in the terrain engine to describe climb difficulty. Different tools can expose different detail levels, but they share the same principle: transparent, deterministic heuristics rather than hidden claims.

Category Thresholds

The race-analysis heuristic uses these planning thresholds:

  • Hors Catégorie (HC): Score ≥ 800 or gain ≥ 1000m.
  • Category 1: Score ≥ 400 or gain ≥ 600m.
  • Category 2: Score ≥ 200 or gain ≥ 350m.
  • Category 3: Score ≥ 100 or gain ≥ 150m, with sufficient length.
  • Category 4: Smaller but still meaningful climbs above the minimum filter.

Geodesic Precision

Gradient calculations depend on horizontal distance. BikeScout uses geodesic distance over the route geometry, so gradient and climb metrics better reflect real-world effort than flat-map approximations.
